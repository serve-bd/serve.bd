---
title: "Status pages"
description: "A public page that shows how your services are doing."
---

A status page shows visitors whether your services work. It lists the services you pick, colored by their uptime checks. It also shows the incidents and maintenance you post. Open **Status pages** in the sidebar.

## Make a page

1. Click **New status page** and give it a name, like **Acme**.
2. The page starts with every service that has an uptime check. Remove the ones visitors should not see.
3. Click **Publish**.

Until you publish, the page is a draft. Only signed-in members of your organization can open it.

The page is at `/status/<address>` on the dashboard's domain. You can also give it its own domain. See [Own domain](#own-domain).

## Components

A component is one line on the page. Each one follows a service's uptime check, or nothing.

- **Follows**: the service whose check sets the color. Pick **Nothing** for a part you report on yourself, like **Support**.
- **Name**: what visitors see. The service's own name and address never show.
- **Group**: components with the same group show under one heading.

Move components up and down with the arrows. A service without an uptime check always shows **Operational**. Set up a check first. See [Monitoring](/docs/monitoring).

## Incidents and maintenance

In the **Incidents** tab:

- **Report incident** posts a problem. Pick the impact and the affected components. The page shows it at once.
- **Update** adds a message. Move the state from **Investigating** to **Identified**, **Monitoring** and **Resolved**. **Resolved** closes the incident.
- **Plan maintenance** sets a start and an end. Visitors see it ahead of time. During the window, its components show **Under maintenance**. Outages found by checks in that window stay off the page.

When a check finds a component down, the page shows the outage by itself. To keep these outages off the page, turn off **Show outages found by checks** in **Design → History**.

## Design

The **Design** tab has a live preview, for desktop and phone. Click **Save** to publish your changes.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Starting point** | Ready-made looks: **Clean**, **Night**, **Editorial** and **Terminal**. Every value stays editable. |
| **Logo** / **Logo on dark** | Shown at the top. The dark one shows when the page is dark. |
| **Accent color** | Links and highlights. Status colors stay green, amber and red. |
| **Theme** | **Visitor's choice** follows the visitor's device. |
| **Font**, **Corners**, **Spacing** | Sans, serif or mono; round or square; roomy or compact. |
| **Daily bars** | One bar per day for 30, 60 or 90 days. Phones show the last 30. |
| **Uptime percent**, **Response time** | Shown under each component. |
| **Past incidents** | How many days of history to list. |
| **Banner** | A note at the top of the page. |
| **Header links**, **Footer text** | Links to your site, support or docs. |
| **Custom CSS** | Extra styles for this page only. Colors are variables like `--sp-bg` and `--sp-ok`. |
| **Hide from search engines** | Asks search engines not to list the page. |

## Who can see it

In **Settings → Who can see it**:

- **Public**: anyone with the address.
- **Password**: visitors type a password once. Their browser remembers it for a month. A new password signs everyone out.
- **Draft**: only signed-in members of your organization.

## Own domain

In **Settings → Own domain**, enter a domain like `status.example.com`. Then pick a **Route**:

- **Public IP**: point an A record for the domain to the dashboard's server. Then pick the **Protocol**:
  - **HTTPS** with **Automatic**: Serve gets a Let's Encrypt certificate once the record points to the server. Caddy and Traefik get their own.
  - **HTTPS** with one of your certificates: pick any certificate of your organization on the dashboard's server that covers the domain. See [Certificates](/docs/domains).
  - **HTTP only**: no certificate.
- **Cloudflare Tunnel**: for a server without a public IP. Pick a tunnel of your organization on the dashboard's server. Serve points the domain at the tunnel in Cloudflare, and Cloudflare serves HTTPS. If the domain already has a record you made yourself, remove it in Cloudflare first.

Click **Check DNS** to see where the domain points. A domain behind Cloudflare's proxy shows Cloudflare's addresses. With the Cloudflare account connected, Serve reads the real record.

The domain shows the status page only. The dashboard and its sign-in page never answer there. An IP allow list on the dashboard does not apply to the status page either.

## Use it elsewhere

Each page also answers at these addresses:

| Address | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| `/badge` | An SVG badge with the overall status. Add `?component=<id>` for one component, and `?label=API` to change its text. |
| `/summary.json` | The status, components, ongoing incidents and planned maintenance as JSON. |
| `/feed.xml` | Incidents and maintenance as RSS. |

A draft or a password page does not answer at these addresses.

## Permissions

The **Status pages** permission lets a member create and change pages and post incidents. Owners, Admins and Developers have it by default. A page can list services from every project, so members limited to some projects cannot manage pages.
